Can you go 20 over in Ontario?

A common question with a short answer. Here’s what the rules say, and what 20 km/h over actually costs on the ticket, in court and on your record.

Short answer

No. Any speed over the posted limit is speeding, and Ontario’s set fines start at 1 km/h over. 20 over is a $95 ticket with 3 demerit points. In a community safety zone it’s $180.

What 20 over costs

Ontario prices a speeding ticket per km/h over the limit. At 20 over you’re in the $3.75 band, and the ticket adds a victim fine surcharge and $5 in costs.

ScaleHow it adds upTotal
On the ticket$75 set fine (20 × $3.75) + $15 surcharge + $5 costs$95
Community safety zone, or construction zone with workers present$150 set fine (20 × $7.50) + $25 surcharge + $5 costs$180
If convicted in court20 × $4.50 (HTA s.128(14)), before any surcharge and costs$90 fine

Either way it carries 3 demerit points, which stay on your record for 2 years from the offence date. Paying the ticket counts as a guilty plea, so the conviction goes on your record too.

Ticket totals are our own maths from Schedule 43 and O. Reg. 161/00; the $5 costs figure comes from secondary sources. Work out any other speed with the Ontario speeding fine calculator.

Where the points start: 16 over

Up to 15 over, a speeding ticket carries no demerit points. At 16 over it carries 3. On the ticket, that one extra km/h adds $2.50 and 3 points.

Is 10 over OK?

It’s still speeding. 10 over is a $40 ticket ($25 set fine, $10 surcharge and $5 costs) with no demerit points. It still goes on your record as a speeding conviction, and insurers price convictions, not points.

What it does to insurance

Insurers look at the conviction, usually for about 3 years. Brokers estimate one speeding ticket adds roughly 10–25% to premiums. That’s a broker estimate, not an official rate.

When speeding becomes stunt driving

Ontario’s stunt-driving rules (O. Reg. 455/07) start well above 20 over, but closer than many drivers think on a highway:

  • 40+ over where the limit is under 80. For example, 90 in a 50.
  • 50+ over on any road. For example, 150 in a 100.
  • 150 km/h or more on any road. On a 110 km/h highway, that’s only 40 over.

At the roadside: an immediate 30-day licence suspension and a 14-day vehicle impoundment, even if the car isn’t yours. If convicted: a $2,000–$10,000 fine (or up to 6 months in jail), 6 demerit points and a 1–3-year licence suspension for a first conviction.

If you’re a G1 or G2 driver

New drivers (G1, G2, M1, M2) get a warning letter at 2–5 points, a second warning at 6–8, and a 60-day suspension at 9. So one 20-over ticket already puts a new driver in the warning range. A speeding conviction for 30+ over means at least a 30-day suspension. More in the demerit points guide.

What’s the limit if there’s no sign?

50 km/h in cities, towns, villages and built-up areas, and 80 km/h elsewhere (Highway Traffic Act, s.128(1)).

School-zone limits depend on the sign. Municipalities can set times or flashing beacons by by-law; with no times on the sign, the limit always applies. In Mississauga, for example, local school zones are 30 km/h Monday to Friday from 6 a.m. to 6 p.m. and 40 km/h otherwise, and they’re community safety zones, so set fines double.

Questions people ask

Can you go 20 over in Ontario?

No. Any speed over the posted limit is speeding. 20 over is a $95 ticket ($75 set fine, $15 victim fine surcharge and $5 costs) and 3 demerit points.

Is there a 10 km/h tolerance in Ontario?

No. Ontario’s set fines start at 1 km/h over. 10 over is a $40 ticket with no demerit points, but it is still a speeding conviction.

How many demerit points is 20 over?

3. Speeding 16 to 29 km/h over carries 3 demerit points, and they stay on your record for 2 years from the offence date.

How much is 20 over in a school zone?

If the school zone is a community safety zone, set fines double and 20 over costs $180 on the ticket ($150 set fine, $25 surcharge and $5 costs). Otherwise it is the usual $95.

Does 20 over affect insurance?

Insurers price speeding convictions, usually for about 3 years. Brokers estimate one ticket adds roughly 10–25% to premiums; that is an estimate, not an official rate.

What is the speed limit in Ontario if it isn’t posted?

50 km/h in cities, towns, villages and built-up areas, and 80 km/h elsewhere (Highway Traffic Act, s.128(1)).

Do school zones apply on weekends?

It depends on the sign. Municipalities can set times or flashing beacons by by-law. With no times on the sign, the school-zone limit always applies.
