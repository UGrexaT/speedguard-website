Ontario speeding fines

What a speeding ticket costs in Ontario, worked out the way the ticket does it: a set fine for each km/h over, plus the victim fine surcharge and $5 in costs.

Short answer

On the ticket, Ontario charges $2.50 per km/h for 1–19 over, $3.75 for 20–29 over and $6.00 for 30–49 over. With the surcharge and costs, 10 over costs $40, 20 over $95 and 30 over $220. At 50+ over there’s no ticket to pay: it goes to court.

Checked October 16, 2026 · Ticket: Schedule 43 · Court: HTA s.128(14)

Set fines on the ticket

The set fine is what you pay if you pay the ticket out of court. It’s charged for every km/h over the limit, and the rate steps up at 20 and 30 over.

If convicted in court

If you take the ticket to trial and are convicted, the fine follows the Highway Traffic Act, s.128(14). These rates are higher than the ticket. They’re fines on conviction, not what you pay out of court.

Your options on a ticket

You usually have 15 days to choose. Paying the ticket is a guilty plea. Option 2 is an early-resolution meeting with a prosecutor. The third option is a trial. If you ignore the ticket, you can be convicted without being there.

Points, stunt driving and insurance

  • Demerit points: 0 for 1–15 over, 3 for 16–29, 4 for 30–49 and 6 for 50+. They stay on your record for 2 years from the offence date. See the demerit points guide.
  • Stunt driving: 40+ over where the limit is under 80, 50+ over anywhere, or 150 km/h or more on any road. At the roadside that means a 30-day licence suspension and a 14-day vehicle impoundment. If convicted: $2,000–$10,000 (or up to 6 months in jail), 6 points and a 1–3-year suspension for a first conviction.
  • New drivers (G1, G2, M1, M2): a speeding conviction for 30+ over means at least a 30-day licence suspension.
  • Insurance: insurers price the conviction, not the points, usually for about 3 years. Brokers estimate one ticket adds roughly 10–25%; treat that as an estimate.

Questions people ask

How much is a speeding ticket in Ontario?

On the ticket, the set fine is $2.50 per km/h over for 1–19 over, $3.75 for 20–29 over and $6.00 for 30–49 over. A victim fine surcharge and $5 in costs are added, so 10 over costs $40, 20 over $95 and 30 over $220 in total. At 50 or more over there is no ticket to pay; it goes to court.

How much is 30 over in Ontario?

$220 on the ticket: a $180 set fine, a $35 victim fine surcharge and $5 in costs, plus 4 demerit points. If convicted in court instead, the fine is $7.00 per km/h over, or $210, before any surcharge and costs.

Are speeding fines doubled in school zones?

Set fines are doubled in community safety zones and in construction zones when workers are present: $5.00, $7.50 or $12.00 per km/h over on the ticket. 20 over in a community safety zone costs $180 in total. In Mississauga, for example, local school zones are community safety zones.

Does paying a speeding ticket count as a conviction?

Yes. Paying the ticket is a guilty plea, so the conviction and any demerit points go on your record. The other options, usually within 15 days, are an early-resolution meeting with a prosecutor (option 2) or a trial. If you ignore the ticket, you can be convicted without being there.

What happens if you go 50 km/h over?

There is no ticket to pay; it goes to court. 50 or more over the limit also counts as stunt driving, which brings an immediate 30-day licence suspension and a 14-day vehicle impoundment at the roadside. If convicted of stunt driving: a $2,000–$10,000 fine, 6 demerit points and a 1–3-year licence suspension for a first conviction.

Is there a grace amount over the speed limit?

No. Any speed over the posted limit is speeding, and Ontario’s set fines start at 1 km/h over.
